Greetings Can,

I'm starting a project I'm calling antipythany, a Python 3.14-compatible
runtime and compiler in Rust : tracing GC instead of refcounting, no GIL,
and two output targets, producing statically-linked native binaries
(Go-style UX) and WasmGC modules. The architecture overlaps yours in pon
substantially: ruff parser pinned to 3.14, a single shared IR, Cranelift as
the backend, tiered execution with tier-0 as the correctness baseline, and
byte-exact differential conformance testing against CPython as the gate.

To the point: pon has no license file, so I am treating your code as
all-rights-reserved: zero lines of pon will be read or copied, and my IR
and codegen are being written clean-room from your public README's
architecture description and from independent designs (kohebi's spec,
Monty, p2w).

However: (1) if you're willing to license pon MIT or Apache-2.0, it would
let me study your tier-1 inline-cache and OSR implementation directly and
save weeks of clean-room rediscovery, and (2) antipythany diverges from pon
enough that I don't think it belongs as a contribution to your repo: the
WasmGC backend, the GC-representation divergence (MMTk rather than Green
Tea), the WasmGC object model, and the uv-integrated build/deps are new
subsystems, not incremental features, and the dual-backend IR contract
shapes every layer differently.

My objective is to be able to run the myriad gobs of python that is filling
up the world due to AI development, without having to run python, and to
hopefully produce something that helps unclutter every machine that runs a
python script. Performance gains are great, too.

If pon's direction and antipythany's converge enough that you'd rather they
stay one project, I'm open to that conversation instead - but the working
assumption is that this diverges enough you might want a separate project
(that cites yours as prior art and stays in clean-room discipline). I'd be
glad to collaborate.

Thank you for your time.

On Fri, 16 Oct 2026 at 08:53, Benjamin Hodgens <ben@hodgens.net> wrote:

> Greetings Can,
>
> I'm starting a project I'm calling antipythany, a Python 3.14-compatible
> runtime and compiler in Rust : tracing GC instead of refcounting, no GIL,
> and two output targets, producing statically-linked native binaries
> (Go-style UX) and WasmGC modules. The architecture overlaps yours in pon
> substantially: ruff parser pinned to 3.14, a single shared IR, Cranelift as
> the backend, tiered execution with tier-0 as the correctness baseline, and
> byte-exact differential conformance testing against CPython as the gate.
>
> To the point: pon has no license file, so I am treating your code as
> all-rights-reserved: zero lines of pon will be read or copied, and my IR
> and codegen are being written clean-room from your public README's
> architecture description and from independent designs (kohebi's spec,
> Monty, p2w).
>
> However: (1) if you're willing to license pon MIT or Apache-2.0, it would
> let me study your tier-1 inline-cache and OSR implementation directly and
> save weeks of clean-room rediscovery, and (2) antipythany diverges from pon
> enough that I don't think it belongs as a contribution to your repo: the
> WasmGC backend, the GC-representation divergence (MMTk rather than Green
> Tea), the WasmGC object model, and the uv-integrated build/deps are new
> subsystems, not incremental features, and the dual-backend IR contract
> shapes every layer differently.
>
> My objective is to be able to run the myriad gobs of python that is
> filling up the world due to AI development, without having to run python,
> and to hopefully produce something that helps unclutter every machine that
> runs a python script. Performance gains are great, too.
>
> If pon's direction and antipythany's converge enough that you'd rather
> they stay one project, I'm open to that conversation instead - but the
> working assumption is that this diverges enough you might want a separate
> project (that cites yours as prior art and stays in clean-room discipline).
> I'd be glad to collaborate.
>
> Thank you for your time.
